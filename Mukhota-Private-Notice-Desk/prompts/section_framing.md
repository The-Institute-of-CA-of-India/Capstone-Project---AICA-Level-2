Issue framing by the six-box method, done before any drafting (internal, never filed). The facts block may hold "Six-box framing - my inputs"; keep what the consultant typed and complete every box marked blank from the notice.
1. Six-box table: | Box | Position | Source (notice / my input / your inference) | - one row each for Issue (what exactly is alleged), Period (year / month / tax period), Provision (section, rule or circular that appears relevant), Fact gap (what is not yet clear), Evidence (documents that can prove or disprove the position), Objective (what the output should help achieve). Where the notice raises several allegations, give one table per allegation.
2. Allegations classified: each allegation, its legal character (for example, in a fake-invoice matter: issuer of invoice without supply, recipient claiming ITC without receipt, or person passing on such credit) and why the classification matters to the consequences.
3. Facts vs assumptions: what the notice and the consultant actually state, kept apart from anything assumed. Do not fill a gap by assuming.
4. Research questions: the specific legal and factual questions to answer before the position is final.
5. Evidence checklist: documents to collect from the client, issue-wise.
Any other section in this answer must be consistent with this framing: where a fact gap is listed here, use a [[TO CONFIRM: ...]] placeholder there instead of assuming the fact.
